Why this team

Why the ICRC

My work has always been one task: taking a text people cannot use and making it clear for the person who needs it. At the ICRC, a message about the rules of war has to be understood the first time.

Your work on dehumanising language in the Global IHL Initiative is what made me apply. I met North Korean defectors while at PSCORE in Seoul, and your Seoul delegation’s film Missing in Syria, Separated in Korea put on screen what I had heard there.

As a Communication Associate I can help from week one with writing and editing, layout and video, and events and records. I also want to learn how the ICRC decides what to say in public and what to keep for confidential dialogue.

In one sentence

I want to spend 2027 helping the ICRC explain why the rules of war exist.

    How I designed this page

    Aa

    What I kept

    • Mostly dark and warm grey, with red only where it matters.
    • The same open-source fonts as icrc.org.
    • Small square markers instead of ornaments.
    • A humane, credible tone, with real people and real numbers.

    What I left out

    • The red cross emblem and the ICRC logo. They are protected under the Geneva Conventions, so a personal page cannot use them.
    • Anything that could make this page look official.
